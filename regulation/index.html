EU regulation

What EU law asks, and where our images help

Placeholder publishes 76 hardened container images. This page maps them to the Cyber Resilience Act, NIS2 and DORA, checked against the Official Journal on 15 November 2026. Using the images does not make your product compliant; the tables show which clauses they help with and which remain your job.

This page is not legal advice, and we make no claim of conformity.

Key dates

Days until the CRA applies

390

11 December 2027. The reporting duties have applied since 11 September 2026.

  1. 17 Oct 2024 NIS2

    Member states’ laws due

    The transposition deadline; national rules apply from 18 October 2024. Essential and important entities must address supply chain security with their direct suppliers, weighing each one’s vulnerabilities and secure development practices (Article 21(2)(d) and 21(3)).

    What the images offer: A page per image with its test results and open findings, for use in a supplier assessment.

  2. 10 Dec 2024 CRA

    The Cyber Resilience Act enters into force

    Regulation (EU) 2024/2847: security requirements for products with digital elements made available on the EU market in the course of a commercial activity, paid or free.

    What the images offer: Annex I mapped clause by clause further down this page, with the gaps shown.

  3. 17 Jan 2025 DORA

    DORA applies to financial entities

    A register of all contractual arrangements for ICT services (Article 28(3)), and exit strategies for the ICT services that support critical or important functions (Article 28(8)).

    What the images offer: Build definitions under EUPL-1.2 and images named by digest. A mirror in your registry keeps working without us.

  4. 11 Sep 2026 CRA

    Reporting obligations apply

    Since this date manufacturers must notify actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents, with an early warning within 24 hours (Article 14). The duty also covers products already on the market (Article 69(3)).

    What the images offer: An SBOM and an OpenVEX statement per release, to tell whether you are affected. Advisories by email and fix times under contract, on a paid plan.

  5. 11 Dec 2027 CRAupcoming

    The Act applies

    Products placed on the market from this date need a conformity assessment and a CE marking. Annex I asks for secure-by-default configuration where applicable, secure distribution of updates and an SBOM covering at least the top-level dependencies. Article 13(8) sets a support period of at least five years, unless the product is expected to be used for less.

    What the images offer: An SBOM, provenance and a scan with each image and release. Each image page states how long that version gets fixes.

Reporting an exploited vulnerability

What Article 14 requires once a vulnerability in your product is exploited, and which image documents help at each step.

Are we affected?

What the Act asks

The 24 hours for the early warning are already running. First you have to find the systems that run the affected version.

What you already have

Query the SBOMs attached to the digests you run. Each lists every package in the image, so you can search them for the affected version.

These duties fall on the manufacturer. If you build a product on these images, that may be you.

Who counts as the manufacturer

No official text mentions container images. This is our reading of the definitions.

  • An image is a product. Software supplied to a user and run on the user’s system is a “product with digital elements” (Article 3(1)).
  • Whoever markets it under their own name is its manufacturer (Article 3(13)), whether for payment or free of charge. Payment decides whether the image is supplied in the course of a commercial activity, and the Commission’s guidance of 27 July 2026 (C(2026) 5252) treats pre-compiled binaries sold for a price as supplied commercially. A paid plan makes us the manufacturer of each image. Article 13(8) sets a support period of five years, or the time a version is expected to be in use where that is shorter; the trust page says how long each version gets fixes.
  • A publisher of free software that is not monetised may fall outside the Act, or be a steward (Articles 3(14) and 24, recital 19). A steward owes a written security policy, cooperation with authorities, and reporting for what it develops.
  • Container runtimes are class II. Annex III lists “Hypervisors and container runtime systems”, so an image whose core is containerd or runc needs a third-party assessment, unless it is free and open source with its technical documentation public (Article 32(3), 32(5)). A Kubernetes control plane is not named.
  • No harmonised standard is cited yet. Until one appears in the Official Journal, nothing gives a presumption of conformity. The first EN 40000 parts are expected in late 2026.
