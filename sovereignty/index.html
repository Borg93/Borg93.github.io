Sovereignty

Own what you run

Software is yours when you can run it, change it and keep it without anyone’s permission. Where it was written matters less than who can switch it off.

Why it matters, and why now

Most of what a cluster runs comes from suppliers outside the EU, on terms they set. One of them changed its terms, and the law has put dates on answering for your supply chain.

    Within weeks

    In 2025 one of the most used free image catalogues went behind a subscription. Whoever pulled from it had to move, or pay.

    What changed

    17 Oct 2024

    NIS2: essential and important entities must address the security of their supply chain, supplier by supplier.

    Directive (EU) 2022/2555, Article 21

    17 Jan 2025

    DORA: financial entities need an exit strategy for the ICT services their critical functions depend on.

    Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, Article 28

    11 Dec 2027

    The CRA applies: an SBOM and security updates for every digital product sold in the EU. Reporting has applied since September 2026.

    Regulation (EU) 2024/2847

What you get

Four ways a supplier can hold you, and what owning the images changes about each.

With a supplier you depend on With images you own

With a supplier you depend on:Terms change overnight

What was free can go behind a subscription, and you find out when a pull fails.

With images you own:Yours to keep

Ordinary OCI images. Copy them into your registry and they stay yours, whatever our terms become.

With a supplier you depend on:Somebody else holds the switch

A licence server, a login, a call home: each is a way to be cut off.

With images you own:Runs with no line out

Nothing calls home or asks a licence server. An image crosses an air gap as a file.

With a supplier you depend on:You cannot say where it came from

An auditor asks what is in your software, and who built it from what.

With images you own:Built in the EU, from source

From each project’s own source, at a commit the catalogue names beside the image.

With a supplier you depend on:Leaving means a migration

The more you build on a supplier, the more it costs to go.

With images you own:Leave in one line

Each image keeps its project’s version tag. Going back is the one line it took to switch.

Owned, not borrowed. Most of the software a cluster runs is governed from the United States, and building it in Europe does not change that. What changes is who you depend on. Your supplier is a company under EU law. The images are copies you hold, and nothing needs our permission to keep running.
